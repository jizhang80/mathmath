## Why arbitration cannot resolve at the spec level

Every candidate resolution of Finding 1 lands outside task 01.5's spec. (a) is refuted by the schema and by L0-3a as implemented: MPM2D/MHF4U cannot be stubs — each would need a strand, an expectation, a non-empty unit **and** at least one node carrying its expectation code, turning the Demo into a four-course bundle and contradicting both the EPIC brief's "two courses" phrasing (`docs/epics/epic-01-core-data-l0-layout-demo-bundle.md:83-84`) and the owner's Demo shape (AMENDMENT-v2.6 §3.1: the tester selects "MTH1W or MCR3U"; a courses-driven picker would gain two throwaway courses). (b) requires editing landed `Core` source and task 01.2's landed spec — outside the arbiter's write authority. (c) — dropping `next_courses` to `[]`, the workaround task 01.2 used for its fixture — directly contradicts the EPIC brief's §4 criterion 8. The root cause is not this spec's wording: it is that `contracts/graph-constraints.md` L0-9's phrase "names existing courses" is ambiguous, one reading was implemented, and that reading invalidates `contracts/examples/courses.json`, a contract artifact in the same signed-off set. Per the arbiter's hard rules, a contract that is ambiguous or self-contradictory is above arbitration.
